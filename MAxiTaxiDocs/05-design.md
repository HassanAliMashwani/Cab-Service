# Design Document
**Project:** Jawad's Project — Wheelchair-Accessible Taxi Website

## 1. Design Direction

Not "generic taxi company." The brand should read as: **premium accessible transportation** — closer to a private chauffeur/healthcare-adjacent service than a budget cab site. Clean, calm, high-contrast, confidence-inspiring — matching a passenger base that includes elderly travelers, medical patients, and international visitors who need to trust the service before they ever speak to a human.

## 2. Visual Principles

- **Clarity over density.** The reference site leans on long paragraphs; this site should favor short statements, icons, and imagery over blocks of text.
- **Accessibility as identity, not an add-on.** Hero imagery and copy should center wheelchair-accessible vehicles and real usage moments (boarding, airport pickup) rather than generic taxi stock photography.
- **High contrast & legible type.** Given the audience (seniors, mobility-impaired passengers, international visitors skimming quickly), body text should default larger and higher-contrast than a typical marketing site.
- **Persistent, unmissable contact.** Call / WhatsApp / Email should be visually dominant — not styled as a subtle secondary link.

## 3. Layout Patterns

### Homepage
1. Sticky header: logo + Call/WhatsApp/Email buttons.
2. Hero: one-sentence value prop, service area, single strong CTA pair (WhatsApp + Call).
3. "How it works" 3-step strip (Enquire → Owner confirms → Fixed-price ride).
4. Service cards (Airport / Medical & NDIS / Local) — icon + one-line description each, linking to full pages.
5. Trust band: insured, police-checked, TUSS + NDIS accepted, years of experience — presented as short credibility chips, not paragraphs.
6. Testimonials carousel.
7. FAQ teaser (3 questions) linking to full FAQ.
8. Footer: full contact block, service-area list, quick links.

### Service Page Template
1. Header image relevant to that service (e.g. airport curb pickup with ramp).
2. Short intro (2–3 sentences max) stating what/who it's for.
3. "What to expect" steps.
4. Equipment/vehicle specifics (ramp type, tie-downs, seating).
5. FAQ specific to that service.
6. CTA block repeated (WhatsApp/Call/Email).

## 4. Color & Type Direction

- Primary palette: a calm, trustworthy color (deep blue or teal) paired with a high-visibility accent (e.g. warm accessible-orange or green) reserved specifically for CTA buttons, so Call/WhatsApp/Email always stand out from the rest of the page.
- Avoid the visual cliché of yellow/black "taxi" branding — reinforces the "premium accessible service" positioning rather than "cheap cab."
- Typography: a clean, highly legible sans-serif; generous line-height and larger base font size than a typical site, given the audience.

## 5. Imagery

- Real (or realistic staged) photos of the accessible vehicle fleet: ramp/lowering-door in use, secured wheelchair, driver assisting a passenger with dignity — not generic taxi stock photos.
- Avoid imagery that overemphasizes disability in a way that feels clinical; aim for warmth and normalcy (a person going about their day, transport as an enabler).


## 6. Accessibility of the Site Itself

- WCAG 2.1 AA target: color contrast ratios, keyboard navigation, alt text on all images, visible focus states, form labels properly associated with inputs.
- Avoid relying on color alone to convey meaning (e.g. form validation states should include icon/text, not just red color).
- Buttons and tap targets sized for easy use on mobile, including for users who may have motor-control difficulty.

## 7. Mobile-First Considerations

- International visitors are likely checking the site on a phone, possibly from an airport or hotel Wi-Fi — prioritize fast load and thumb-reachable CTAs (sticky bottom bar with Call/WhatsApp on mobile is worth considering).
- Forms should be short and mobile-friendly (large inputs, minimal typing — use selects/toggles where possible for fields like "wheelchair type").

## 8. Deliverables for Next Stage

- Low-fidelity wireframes: homepage, flagship wheelchair page, enquiry form.
- Component list: header/nav, hero, service card, trust chip, testimonial card, FAQ accordion, CTA band, footer.
- Style tile: color palette, type scale, button states, icon set — before full high-fidelity mockups.
